Electromagnetism

Maxwell's Equations

Four laws for the electric and magnetic fields, given in integral and differential form. Gaussian units are used throughout, with SI at the end.

1

Gauss's law

The electric flux leaving a closed surface is proportional to the charge inside.

\oint_{\partial\Omega}\vec E\cdot d\vec a = 4\pi\int_{\Omega}\rho\,dV \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \nabla\cdot\vec E = 4\pi\rho
2

No magnetic monopoles

The total magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero.

\oint_{\partial\Omega}\vec B\cdot d\vec a = 0 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \nabla\cdot\vec B = 0
3

Faraday's law of induction

The EMF around a closed loop equals minus the rate of change of the magnetic flux through it.

\oint_{\partial\Sigma}\vec E\cdot d\vec l = -\frac{1}{c}\frac{d}{dt}\int_\Sigma\vec B\cdot d\vec a \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \nabla\times\vec E = -\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\vec B}{\partial t}
4

Ampère–Maxwell law

The magnetic field around a closed loop is set by the current through it plus the displacement current, which is the changing electric flux.

\oint_{\partial\Sigma}\vec B\cdot d\vec l = \frac{1}{c}\left(4\pi\int_\Sigma\vec J\cdot d\vec a+\frac{d}{dt}\int_\Sigma\vec E\cdot d\vec a\right) \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \nabla\times\vec B = \frac{1}{c}\left(4\pi\vec J+\frac{\partial\vec E}{\partial t}\right)

Each integral form turns into its differential form through the divergence theorem (1, 2) or Stokes' theorem (3, 4), applied to an arbitrarily small volume or surface.

Two consequences

Charge conservation

Take the divergence of law 4. Since \nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\vec B)=0, and law 1 gives \nabla\cdot\vec E = 4\pi\rho,

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\vec J = 0 .

Without Maxwell's displacement-current term this would be violated whenever charge builds up, for example on a charging capacitor.

Light is an electromagnetic wave

In vacuum (\rho=0,\ \vec J=0), take the curl of law 3 and use \nabla\times(\nabla\times\vec E)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec E)-\nabla^2\vec E:

\nabla^2\vec E = \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2\vec E}{\partial t^2}, \qquad \nabla^2\vec B = \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2\vec B}{\partial t^2}.

Both fields obey a wave equation with speed c.

SI units

\begin{aligned} \nabla\cdot\vec E &= \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0} &\qquad \nabla\cdot\vec B &= 0 \\[4pt] \nabla\times\vec E &= -\frac{\partial\vec B}{\partial t} &\qquad \nabla\times\vec B &= \mu_0\vec J + \mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial\vec E}{\partial t} \end{aligned}

with c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}. Converting from Gaussian units: \vec E_G = \sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\vec E_{SI}, \vec B_G = \sqrt{4\pi/\mu_0}\,\vec B_{SI} and \rho_G = \rho_{SI}/\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0}.
