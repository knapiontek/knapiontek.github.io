Quantum mechanics

Dirac Equation

A relativistic wave equation for spin-½ particles, first order in both space and time.

Hamiltonian form

\left(\beta mc^2 + c\sum_{n=1}^{3}\alpha_n p_n\right)\psi(\mathbf x,t) = i\hbar\,\frac{\partial \psi(\mathbf x,t)}{\partial t}, \qquad p_n = -i\hbar\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_n}

Here \psi is a four-component spinor and \alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3,\beta are 4\times4 matrices. Squaring the operator on the left must give the relativistic energy relation E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4, which forces

\alpha_i^2 = \beta^2 = I,\qquad \alpha_i\alpha_j + \alpha_j\alpha_i = 0\;(i\ne j),\qquad \alpha_i\beta + \beta\alpha_i = 0 .

Dirac representation

\beta = \begin{pmatrix} I_2 & 0 \\ 0 & -I_2 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \alpha_i = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \sigma_i \\ \sigma_i & 0 \end{pmatrix}

with the Pauli matrices

\sigma_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},\quad \sigma_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -i \\ i & 0 \end{pmatrix},\quad \sigma_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.

Covariant form

Multiplying by \beta/c and defining \gamma^0=\beta,\ \gamma^i=\beta\alpha_i gives the compact, manifestly Lorentz-covariant form

\left(i\hbar\,\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - mc\right)\psi = 0, \qquad \{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu} I_4

with metric signature \eta = \mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1) and \partial_0 = \tfrac1c\partial_t.

Free-particle solutions

For a particle at rest (\mathbf p = 0) the equation reduces to \beta mc^2\psi = i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi, so

\psi = \begin{pmatrix}\chi\\0\end{pmatrix} e^{-imc^2t/\hbar} \quad\text{or}\quad \psi = \begin{pmatrix}0\\ \chi\end{pmatrix} e^{+imc^2t/\hbar},

with energies E = \pm mc^2. The two components of \chi are the two spin states; the negative-energy branch was Dirac's prediction of the positron.
