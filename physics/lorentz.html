Special relativity

Lorentz Transformation

The transformation between inertial frames follows from one postulate: light moves at the same speed in every one of them.

Derivation

Take two inertial frames S and S'. Their origins coincide at t=t'=0, and S' moves along x with velocity v. The Galilean transformation

x' = x - vt,\qquad x = x' + vt'\quad(t'=t)

would make a light pulse move at c-v in S', which contradicts observation. Keep the transformation linear, so that uniform motion stays uniform, but allow a scale factor \gamma:

x' = \gamma\,(x - vt),\qquad x = \gamma\,(x' + vt').

The first equation puts the origin of S' at x = vt. The second is the same statement seen from S', where S moves at -v. By symmetry, \gamma must be the same in both.

Now send a light pulse from the common origin. Both frames must measure speed c, so x = ct and x' = ct':

ct' = \gamma\,(c - v)\,t,\qquad ct = \gamma\,(c + v)\,t'.

Multiply the two equations and cancel tt':

c^2 = \gamma^2\,(c^2 - v^2)\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}.

Eliminating x' between the two transformation equations gives the rule for time:

x' = \gamma\,(x - vt),\qquad t' = \gamma\left(t - \frac{v\,x}{c^2}\right),\qquad \gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}

Invariant interval and time dilation

A direct substitution shows that the Lorentz transformation preserves the spacetime interval:

s^2 = c^2t'^2 - x'^2 = c^2t^2 - x^2 .

Take a clock at rest in S' (x'=0), which moves at v in S (x = vt):

c^2t'^2 = c^2t^2 - v^2t^2 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad t' = t\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}} = \frac{t}{\gamma}.

The moving clock records less time: t = \gamma\,t'.

Low-speed limit

With \beta = v^2/c^2, expand \gamma(\beta) = (1-\beta)^{-1/2} in a Taylor series about \beta = 0:

\gamma'(\beta) = \tfrac12(1-\beta)^{-3/2},\quad \gamma'(0) = \tfrac12 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \gamma \approx 1 + \frac{\beta}{2} = 1 + \frac{v^2}{2c^2}
E = \gamma mc^2 \approx mc^2 + \tfrac12 mv^2

The rest energy plus the Newtonian kinetic energy.

Minkowski diagram

Lines of constant s^2 are hyperbolae. Inside the light cone (s^2>0) intervals are timelike: some frame sees both events at the same place. Outside (s^2<0) they are spacelike: some frame sees them at the same time. A boost tilts the ct' and x' axes symmetrically towards the light line, and every event stays on its own hyperbola.
