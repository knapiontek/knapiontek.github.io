Classical mechanics

Newtonian Gravity

Newton's second law plus the inverse-square law, stepped forward in time for a small stellar system. Bodies that collide merge.

The two laws

\vec F = m\,\frac{d^2\vec u}{dt^2} \qquad\qquad \vec F = -\,G\frac{Mm}{r^2}\,\hat{\mathbf r} = -\,G\frac{Mm}{r^3}\,\vec r

Here \vec r points from the attracting mass M to m. The minus sign makes gravity attractive.

Stellar system

Solution method

In components, with \vec u = (x, y) the position relative to the attracting mass and r^2 = x^2 + y^2:

\frac{F_x}{F} = \frac{x}{r},\qquad \frac{F_y}{F} = \frac{y}{r} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \vec a = \frac{\vec F}{m} = -\,GM\,\frac{\vec u}{r^3}

For many bodies, the accelerations add up:

\vec a_i = \sum_{j\ne i} G\,m_j\,\frac{\vec u_j - \vec u_i}{\lvert\vec u_j - \vec u_i\rvert^3}

Each time step first computes every acceleration from the current positions, then updates velocities and positions. This is semi-implicit Euler: the new velocity moves the body, which keeps orbits from slowly spiralling outward.

\begin{aligned} \vec v_{n+1} &= \vec v_n + \vec a_n\,\Delta t &&= \vec v_0 + \sum_{k=0}^{n}\vec a_k\,\Delta t \\ \vec u_{n+1} &= \vec u_n + \vec v_{n+1}\,\Delta t &&= \vec u_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\vec v_k\,\Delta t \end{aligned}

When two bodies touch, they merge in a perfectly inelastic collision. Mass (volume) and momentum are conserved:

m = m_1 + m_2,\qquad \vec v = \frac{m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2}{m_1 + m_2},\qquad r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3m}{4\pi}}
