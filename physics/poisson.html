Fields · finite differences

Poisson Equation

The equation behind electrostatics, gravity and steady heat flow, solved on a grid in one and two dimensions.

\nabla^2 u = f

One dimension

\frac{d^2u}{dx^2} = f(x)

Second derivative from Taylor series

\begin{aligned} u(x-h) &= u(x) - h\,u'(x) + \tfrac{1}{2}h^2u''(x) - \tfrac16 h^3u'''(x) + \mathcal{O}(h^4) \\[4pt] u(x+h) &= u(x) + h\,u'(x) + \tfrac{1}{2}h^2u''(x) + \tfrac16 h^3u'''(x) + \mathcal{O}(h^4) \end{aligned}

Adding the two cancels the odd terms:

u(x-h) + u(x+h) = 2u(x) + h^2u''(x) + \mathcal{O}(h^4) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u''(x) = \frac{u(x-h) - 2u(x) + u(x+h)}{h^2} + \mathcal{O}(h^2)

The same result from the definition

\begin{aligned} u'_-(x) &= \frac{u(x)-u(x-h)}{h}, \qquad u'_+(x) = \frac{u(x+h)-u(x)}{h} \\[4pt] u''(x) &\approx \frac{u'_+(x)-u'_-(x)}{h} = \frac{u(x-h)-2u(x)+u(x+h)}{h^2} \end{aligned}

Finite-difference system

On nodes x_n with spacing h, writing u_n \approx u(x_n) and f_n = f(x_n):

u_{n-1} - 2u_n + u_{n+1} = h^2 f_n

For five interior nodes, with boundary values u_0 = c_0 and u_6 = c_6 moved to the right-hand side:

\begin{bmatrix} -2 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} u_1 \\ u_2 \\ u_3 \\ u_4 \\ u_5 \end{bmatrix} = h^2 \begin{bmatrix} f_1 \\ f_2 \\ f_3 \\ f_4 \\ f_5 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} c_0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ c_6 \end{bmatrix}

Example with an exact solution

\frac{d^2u}{dx^2} = e^{-x^2}\left(4x^2 - 2\right),\qquad -3 \le x \le 3

Integrate once, using integration by parts, \int u'\,v\,dx = uv - \int u\,v'\,dx, on the 4x^2e^{-x^2} term with v = -2x and u' = -2xe^{-x^2}:

\begin{aligned} \frac{du}{dx} &= \int 4x^2e^{-x^2}dx - \int 2e^{-x^2}dx \\ &= \Bigl(-2xe^{-x^2} + \int 2e^{-x^2}dx\Bigr) - \int 2e^{-x^2}dx = -2xe^{-x^2} + C_1 \end{aligned}

Integrate again with the substitution w = -x^2:

u = \int -2xe^{-x^2}dx = e^{-x^2} + C_1x + C_2

The boundary values u(\pm3) = e^{-9} give C_1 = C_2 = 0, so u = e^{-x^2}.

Two dimensions

\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}+\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial y^2} = f(x,y)

Apply the one-dimensional stencil in each direction and add (the five-point stencil):

\nabla^2 u \approx \frac{u(x-h,y) + u(x+h,y) + u(x,y-h) + u(x,y+h) - 4u(x,y)}{h^2}
u_{i-1,j} + u_{i+1,j} + u_{i,j-1} + u_{i,j+1} - 4u_{i,j} = h^2 f_{i,j}

On a 3\times3 interior grid with zero boundary values, numbering nodes row by row:

\left[\begin{array}{ccc|ccc|ccc} -4 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & -4 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -4 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \hline 1 & 0 & 0 & -4 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 1 & -4 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 1 & -4 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ \hline 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & -4 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 1 & -4 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 1 & -4 \end{array}\right] \begin{bmatrix} u_{11} \\ u_{12} \\ u_{13} \\ u_{21} \\ u_{22} \\ u_{23} \\ u_{31} \\ u_{32} \\ u_{33} \end{bmatrix} = h^2 \begin{bmatrix} f_{11} \\ f_{12} \\ f_{13} \\ f_{21} \\ f_{22} \\ f_{23} \\ f_{31} \\ f_{32} \\ f_{33} \end{bmatrix}

The matrix is banded: no entry lies more than n places from the diagonal. Gaussian elimination restricted to the band costs \mathcal{O}(n^4) rather than \mathcal{O}(n^6), so fine grids still solve instantly.
