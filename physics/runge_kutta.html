Numerical methods

Runge–Kutta Methods

From the first-order Euler step to the classic fourth-order scheme, tested on a driven oscillator whose exact solution is known.

First order: the Euler step (RK1)

y' = f(t, y)

Truncate the Taylor series after the linear term:

y_{n+1} = y_n + \Delta t\,y'(t_n) + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t^2) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad y_{n+1} = y_n + \Delta t\,f(t_n, y_n)

Each step has error \mathcal{O}(\Delta t^2). Reaching a fixed time takes 1/\Delta t steps, so the total error is \mathcal{O}(\Delta t).

Fourth order (RK4)

Sample the slope four times inside the step and take a weighted average. The weights cancel the Taylor error terms up to \Delta t^4:

\begin{aligned} k_1 &= f(t_n,\; y_n) \\ k_2 &= f(t_n + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2},\; y_n + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}k_1) \\ k_3 &= f(t_n + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2},\; y_n + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}k_2) \\ k_4 &= f(t_n + \Delta t,\; y_n + \Delta t\,k_3) \\[4pt] y_{n+1} &= y_n + \tfrac{\Delta t}{6}\left(k_1 + 2k_2 + 2k_3 + k_4\right) \end{aligned}

Local error \mathcal{O}(\Delta t^5), global error \mathcal{O}(\Delta t^4). Halving the step makes the result about 16 times more accurate, for four function evaluations per step.

Numerical example

y'' + 2y' + 3y = 2\cos\left(\omega t-\frac{\pi}{3}\right)

Look for the steady-state solution

y = a\cos\omega t + b\sin\omega t

Expand the right-hand side as 2\cos\frac{\pi}{3}\cos\omega t + 2\sin\frac{\pi}{3}\sin\omega t = \cos\omega t + \sqrt3\sin\omega t and substitute:

\begin{aligned} y' &= -a\omega\sin\omega t + b\omega\cos\omega t \\ y'' &= -a\omega^2\cos\omega t - b\omega^2\sin\omega t \\[4pt] y'' + 2y' + 3y &= \bigl[(3-\omega^2)a + 2\omega b\bigr]\cos\omega t + \bigl[-2\omega a + (3-\omega^2)b\bigr]\sin\omega t \end{aligned}

Match the coefficients of \cos and \sin:

\begin{cases} (3-\omega^2)a + 2\omega b = 1 \\ -2\omega a + (3-\omega^2)b = \sqrt{3} \end{cases}

At resonance, \omega=\sqrt{3}, the diagonal terms vanish:

2\sqrt{3}\,b = 1,\quad -2\sqrt{3}\,a = \sqrt{3} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad a = -\tfrac12,\quad b = \tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}
\begin{cases} y = -\frac{1}{2}\cos\sqrt{3}t + \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\sin\sqrt{3}t \\[6pt] y' = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin\sqrt{3}t + \frac{1}{2}\cos\sqrt{3}t \end{cases}

Starting from y(0) = -\tfrac12 and y'(0) = \tfrac12 the homogeneous part is zero, so this is the exact solution for all t.

As a first-order system

\mathbf u = \begin{bmatrix} y \\ x \end{bmatrix},\qquad \mathbf u' = \mathbf f(t,\mathbf u) = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ -2x - 3y + 2\cos(\sqrt3\,t - \frac{\pi}{3}) \end{bmatrix}

Both methods advance the vector \mathbf u with the formulas above.
