Quantum mechanics

Schrödinger Equation

How a non-relativistic wave function evolves in time, and what its stationary states look like.

Time-dependent form

i\hbar\,\frac{\partial\psi(\mathbf x,t)}{\partial t} = \hat H\psi = \left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2 + V(\mathbf x)\right]\psi(\mathbf x,t)

The same wave function \psi appears on both sides. With V=0 this is the free-particle equation -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi = i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi.

Stationary states

If the potential does not depend on time, try separating variables \psi(\mathbf x,t) = \phi(\mathbf x)\,e^{-iEt/\hbar}. The time derivative gives a factor E, leaving the eigenvalue problem

-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\phi + V\phi = E\,\phi

The probability density |\psi|^2 = |\phi|^2 of such a state does not change in time.

Example: particle in a box

Take V=0 for 0<x<L and infinite walls outside, so \phi(0)=\phi(L)=0. The equation \phi'' = -k^2\phi with k^2 = 2mE/\hbar^2 has solutions

\phi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\,\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L},\qquad E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2},\qquad n = 1,2,3,\dots
