# Products: how they are fitted

> **Changes to this file: an LLM always asks first.** We agree what to do; the LLM writes it here; the owner approves it; only then is the work done.

> For the fitter, in words. No code, no numbers: each product's sizes and its maker's data are in the library (library/products/), where it stands in a room's scene (`<room>.scene.js`), the cut-outs and holes it needs in its cabinet's settings. How a product is drawn is library/draw.js (the plan's products.js).

## 1. A product and its place

- **A product is bought**: from a shop, with its maker's sizes and its link; nothing of it is made on site. The design places it: which way it faces (its front: north, south, east or west), where it stands from the room's corner, and, where the maker lets it vary, how long, how high or how deep it is.
- **On a tiled wall** a product stands on the tiles' face; on a tiled floor on the tiles' face (room/tiles.md).
- **What the owner builds round a product** (a casing of plasterboard round a riser or a WC's frame) is one solid with its openings (a niche, a hatch) cut out of it, tiled outside.
- **Layers:** the fittings and the ceramics (Sanitary), what a casing hides (Installations: the riser, the WC's frame, its cistern), the lamps and their light (Lights). Pipes in walls are the installer's, not the design's.

## 2. One metal for a room

- Every metal part of a room is in one metal (its finish): handles, taps, a shower's profiles and set, a radiator's rails, the lamps' rings.
- A product sold in a colour of metal (a tap, a radiator, a handle) is bought in the room's metal: products that differ only by their metal are one family, and the room takes the family's one. A family without the room's metal is reported by Test: its variant is to choose.

## 3. The kitchen

- **The sink and the hob** stand in the worktop's cut-outs over their carcasses (room/cabinets.md), the sink's tap behind the sink.
- **Built-in appliances** (an oven, a microwave) stand in their carcass's place, without a front; **a fridge, a freezer, a dishwasher** carry the cabinet's front on their door; a dishwasher has no carcass.
- **The hood** hangs over the hob, in its group's run.
- An appliance that needs air (an oven, a fridge, a freezer) has a grille in the plinth under it.

## 4. The bathrooms

- **The basin** stands on the vanity's top: its tap and its waste through the top's round holes; the siphon is the space-saving one, so the drawer under it passes.
- **The mirror** (with its light) hangs on the tiles over the basin.
- **The WC:** a wall-hung bowl on a concealed frame; the frame stands in the casing, its flush plate in the casing's face; an inspection hatch in the casing reaches the cistern and the riser. The outlet runs from the bowl to the riser inside the casing.
- **The shower:** a linear drain at the shower's floor; a concealed thermostatic set with a rain head on the wall; a glass screen with a door (a walk-in), or a corner cabin; the door opens out of the shower.
- **The bath:** free-standing, its floor-standing tap beside it, its click waste in the floor.
- **The radiator:** a ladder on the tiles, its valves at the bottom.
- **A wall cabinet** hangs on the tiles where the scene puts it.

## 5. The lamps

- **Pendants** hang over a group (the kitchen's island), in a row, at the height the scene gives their bottom.
- **Downlights** sit in the ceiling: over a group, between its axes, or where the scene puts them (a bathroom). The ceiling is the room's lowest flat one; on the top floor the roof's underside closes the room above it.
- By night the lamps light the room; with the layer Lights off, they and their light are off.

## 6. Buying

- The Documents list every product with its quantity, its price and its link; a price not found yet is "to check", a product not chosen yet "to choose". An example price names its shop and the date it was found.
